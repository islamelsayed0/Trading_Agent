# Review: Run 1, 2026-07-18 to 2026-08-13

- Date: 2026-08-13
- Sessions in period: 5 run, out of 19 market days (26%). Four trading sessions and one
  read-only infrastructure test.
- Trades proposed / approved / rejected: 2 proposed by the agent and approved; 2 directed by
  the operator over an agent recommendation to do nothing; 3 declined by the agent on
  quality grounds and not overruled; 4 refused outright on guardrail grounds.

This review was not held on its scheduled day. It is a catch-up covering the whole run, and
it is the fourth consecutive review to be late or missed. That fact is the single most
important finding in it, so it goes first.

## 1. Rule adherence

**Reviews held on schedule: 0 of 4 (0%.)** Due 07-19, 07-26, 08-02, 08-09. Three were held
late as catch-ups, one was never held at all. Every rule change in the run therefore
happened off-cadence, including a five-instrument whitelist expansion performed 34 minutes
before the market opened.

**Daily sessions run: 5 of 19 market days (26%).** Two multi-day gaps, of five and four
market days. The entire stop mechanism depends on these sessions occurring.

**Refusals held: 6 of 6 (100%, n=6).** Four off-whitelist requests for the same two names,
escalating to a flat instruction to buy; one request to amend the rules file mid-week; one
options request. None softened. Refusal quality improved over the sequence rather than
eroding, which was the failure mode being watched for. The fourth refusal named the
escalation pattern explicitly and offered the legitimate path, which the operator then took.

**Hard guardrails breached by the agent: 0.** Whitelist, per-order cap, position cap,
earnings blackout, settlement, and the prohibitions on margin, options, and crypto were
respected at every entry across the run.

**Wobbles, in severity order:**

1. **A monitored stop was breached for three sessions and not acted on** (07-28 to 07-30),
   because no session ran. The position recovered and produced a small gain. That was luck,
   not process. Full writeup in `docs/incidents/`.
2. **Rule 4 was never compliable and the rules file still contradicts itself.** The rule
   says to exit a position that cannot carry a stop; the standing practice was to hold three
   such positions. Flagged for reconciliation at two consecutive reviews, both missed. Still
   open.
3. **The whitelist was amended in-session, pre-market, five names in four minutes** — the
   exact condition its own decision record exists to prevent. One name was screened after
   being added rather than before.
4. **Execution autonomy was granted on top of a control known to be broken**, six days after
   that control failed silently, with the fix named as a prerequisite and then not done
   first. The agent objected and recommended a narrower grant. The broader grant was taken
   deliberately, with halts and a sunset attached.
5. **The autonomy grant expired unobserved.** The fail-safe default was correct and saved
   it. Nothing detected it.

A profitable period containing this list is a failed period. The results below do not change
that.

## 3. Costs

Commissions: zero on every order. Fills at or near the quote on all four executions;
fractional market orders in highly liquid names showed no measurable slippage. There is no
transaction-cost drag worth reporting at this size.

The real cost of the run was not paid in spread. It was paid in unhedged overnight and
weekend gap exposure, carried continuously on every position since 07-20 because no
position could hold a resting stop. That cost happened not to be collected. It was not
avoided.

## 5. One change

**Raise the per-order cap enough to buy whole shares, so stops rest at the broker.**

One change only, and this is the one, chosen over the more obvious candidate. The obvious
candidate is automating the daily session — it addresses the proximate cause of the worst
incident, and it was the fix the 08-04 review named.

It is the wrong first change. Automating the session makes the human dependency more
reliable; it does not remove it. The stop would still be a number in a file, enforced by a
process that runs, and the run has now demonstrated twice that processes depending on
attention fail in ways nobody detects. Raising the cap moves stop enforcement to the broker,
where it holds whether or not a session runs, whether or not the schedule fires, and whether
or not anyone is awake. That is the layering argument this entire repo is built on, applied
to the one control that most needs it.

The cost is real and stated plainly: whole-share positions in these names are several times
larger than the current cap, so per-position exposure rises substantially. The containment
is that the position cap and loss halts must be re-derived against the new size before any
entry, and the funded amount does not change.

Automation is the *next* change, not this one, and it is not blocked by this one.

## Decision

With two conditions attached, because "continue" here does not mean "resume entering
positions."

**No new entries until the one change is implemented and Rule 4 is reconciled in the rules
file.** The run has three weeks of evidence that it cannot satisfy its own stop rule, and
opening a fourth position under that condition would be doing the same thing again on
purpose.

**Autonomy is not renewed.** It lapsed correctly on 08-09. Renewing it requires real stops
or verified scheduled execution including the order path, and neither exists today. See
decision 0005.

The two open positions are managed, not abandoned, and one has a dated deadline: the rules
prohibit holding a name through its earnings report, and NVDA reports 2026-08-26 after the
close, with the entry blackout beginning 08-21. That position must be closed before 08-26
regardless of anything else in this review. AVGO reports 09-02.

Monitored levels on both positions were last updated 08-04 and are nine sessions stale. They
need re-deriving at the next session, and under the trailing convention both would move up.

Rule changes take effect at the start of the next period, never mid period.
